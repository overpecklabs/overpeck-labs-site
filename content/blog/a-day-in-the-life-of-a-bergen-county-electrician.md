*Charlie and his company, Your Electric Co., are a fictional sample business we made up for this video. The customers and jobs you see are sample data, not real clients or testimonials.*

## 6:00 AM: Charlie starts the day

This is Charlie. He runs his own electrical business in Bergen County. Before the sun is fully up, he's in the driveway with a coffee, loading the van.

If you run a trade business, you already know the shape of this day. The work happens with your hands, on a ladder, in an attic or behind a panel. The business side (phones, estimate requests, scheduling, follow-ups) happens in the gaps, and it can end up waiting until the evening.

The video follows Charlie through one day and shows five small systems that can run in the background while he works. None of them replaces Charlie. They take repeat tasks off his plate.

## 9:40 AM: Up a ladder

Charlie is on a step ladder installing a ceiling fixture. Both hands are on the fixture, and his phone buzzes in his tool pouch. He doesn't look at it, and that's the right call. Answering a phone halfway up a ladder isn't safe.

This is where the **AI phone receptionist** comes in. When Charlie's busy, it can pick up the call, talk to the customer in a natural voice and ask what they need. In the video, a customer named Maria says she needs two outlets added in her kitchen. The receptionist offers tomorrow at 2 PM, Maria agrees, and the job is booked. A moment later Charlie gets a text summary: the time, the job, the customer's name and town, and a note that the work is near the sink in an older home.

Charlie reads it when he's down off the ladder and already knows what's coming tomorrow. We'll go deeper on how this works in an upcoming post about the AI phone receptionist.

## 1:30 PM: A website that does something

At lunch, a homeowner named Jordan finds Charlie's website and fills out the estimate form: name, town, phone and a description of the job (an EV charger install). He taps send.

Two things happen right away. Jordan gets a text and an email confirming the request, so he knows it landed. And Charlie's phone gets a "New estimate request" alert. Speed matters here. Jordan hears back quickly, and Charlie doesn't have to stop what he's doing to make that happen.

## 4:05 PM: The website chat

Later, a visitor asks the website chat whether Charlie installs EV chargers in Ridgewood. The chat answers the question: yes, home EV chargers across Bergen County. Then it points the visitor to the next step, which is the estimate request form.

That's deliberate. The chat answers common questions and sends people to the form. It doesn't book jobs or quote prices. Booking and pricing stay with Charlie. An upcoming post covers the form and the follow-up behind it.

## 5:00 PM: Confirmations and reminders

As the day winds down, tomorrow's jobs get their paperwork handled. Customers receive a confirmation text ("Confirmed: tomorrow at 2:00 PM with Your Electric Co. See you then!") and a reminder text the day before the visit. Those small messages mean fewer surprises, and Charlie doesn't have to type any of them.

## 5:40 PM: An urgent call after hours

At 5:40, a different customer, Chris, calls after hours. He smells something burning near his electrical panel. The AI receptionist responds with safety first: don't touch the panel, and if there's smoke or sparks, or the smell gets stronger, get everyone out and call 911. It also flags the call as urgent for Charlie right away, with a **Call back** option on his phone.

An AI receptionist should never play electrician. In this moment its job is to give calm, safe instructions and get the right information to the right person quickly. Anything involving real danger belongs with 911 and a licensed professional. An upcoming post walks through that after-hours flow, plus confirmations.

## 6:30 PM: Dinner

The last scene is the point of the whole video. Charlie is at the dinner table with his wife, daughter and son, laughing over spaghetti. His phone lies face-up and dark. Nobody's checking it.

While he eats, a silent card arrives: a new job booked for tomorrow at 3:30 PM, a ceiling fan for Linda in Fair Lawn, added below the 9:00, 11:30 and 2:00 jobs already on his schedule. The business keeps booking jobs while Charlie is with his family. Thanks to Overpeck Labs, Charlie can enjoy what matters: his family time.

## What this means for your business

Charlie's day comes down to five jobs he'd otherwise do by hand:

- **Answering the phone** when you're busy, and getting the job details down. That's the AI phone receptionist.
- **Responding to estimate requests quickly.** That's the website form with an automatic text and email.
- **Answering simple questions on your site** and guiding people to the form. That's the website chat.
- **Confirming and reminding** so jobs show up on schedule and people know who's coming.
- **Flagging urgent calls after hours** with safety-first instructions, so the serious ones reach you right away.

If you have a full-time office person handling all of this, you may not need much of it. If it's mostly you and a phone, it can change how your evenings look.
